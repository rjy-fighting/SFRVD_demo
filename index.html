Abstract Moiré patterns caused by frequency aliasing between digital displays and camera sensors severely degrade video quality. Compared with image demoiréing, video demoiréing is more challenging due to temporally inconsistent moiré degradations, which make neighboring frames unreliable references and easily lead to temporally inconsistent restoration. Existing video demoiréing methods mainly improve temporal stability by aggregating adjacent frames, but they remain limited by unreliable inter-frame cues and the spatial entanglement of moiré artifacts with image structures and fine details. To address these challenges, we propose the spectrum-guided state-space modeling for raw video demoiréing (S3RVD), which combines spectrum-guided moiré discrimination with spatial-temporal state-space modeling, enabling reliable temporal cues selection while suppressing spatially entangled moiré without explicit spatial separation. In the high-frequency branch, the Spectrum-Modulation Spatial Temporal (SMST) group integrates direction-aware spatial-temporal scanning with spectrum-guided modulation, allowing reliable spatial-temporal detail cues to be selectively propagated while suppressing temporally unstable moiré interference. In the low-frequency branch, the Multi-Scale Spatial Temporal (MSST) group captures multi-scale local interactions to suppress moiré disturbances entangled with structures and preserve structural consistency. Extensive experiments demonstrate that our S3RVD achieves state-of-the-art quantitative and qualitative performance in video demoiréing, delivering effective moiré suppression and strong temporal consistency.
